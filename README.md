Introduction:

Candida auris is a fungus with multi-drug resistance. 
This project focuses on the genes and the resulting protein-protein interactions responsible for conferring azole resistance to the organism.

Methodology:

1. A comprehensive literature review was conducted to identify the most important genes responsible for azole resistance in C auris.

2. NCBI-gene and protein databases were used to obtain the properties of the corresponding proteins coded by the genes.

3. PPI analysis was performed in STRING, with first-shell interactors.

4. STRING data put in cytoscape to obtain other important parameters- dergree, closeness centrality, betweenness centrality and clustering coefficient

5. STRING IDs of 1st shell interactors were converted into actual protein names.

6. Gene ontology(GO) analysis was performed in cytoscape and GO plots were obtained using R.

Results:

1. 5 genes were noted in the majority of literature: Erg11, Mdr1, Tac1b, Cdr1 and Upc2

2. Mdr1 and Tac1b are not found in the same PPI network as the other 3 genes; less evidence is available in NCBI to elaborate on the functions of these 2 genes.

3. Erg11 has the highest degree (9) along with some 1st shell interactors

4. GO analysis was conducted from which- 4 enriched BP terms, 9 enriched MF terms and 1 enriched CC terms were obtained

5. Heatmap analysis showed that the genes involved in ergosterol and lipid biosynthesis were clustered together, and genes involved in xenobiotic degradation and chemical responses were clustered together.
